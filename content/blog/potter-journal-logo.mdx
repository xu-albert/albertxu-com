## The one thing I couldn't have

Before any of the drawing, there was a constraint that set the terms for everything after it.

**Flutter's canvas cannot stroke a path with varying width.** `drawPath` with a stroke paint gives you one uniform thickness for the whole line. That sounds like a small thing until you think about what makes a drawn line look drawn: pressure. The stroke swells where the pen presses and thins where it lifts. That's the entire visual signature of ink, and it was simply not available.

So every bit of "hand-drawn" had to come from geometry instead — the shape of the silhouette, a rim that isn't level, a foot that isn't symmetric, a deliberate gap where the stroke opens and closes. That constraint is the spine of everything below. Most of the rounds are, in one way or another, about buying back with shape what I couldn't buy with pressure.

## You can't walk around a vase

Round 5 came from a note I gave: make it look like it's "viewed from up top and to the side."

<Figure
  items={[
    {

Half of that instruction is a no-op. **A vase is rotationally symmetric**, so orbiting it horizontally changes nothing — move to the side of a bud vase and you get an identical silhouette. Only raising your eye level (which opens the rim ellipse) or tilting the vase itself changes what you see.

Naming that saved a round. What it produced instead was a new problem: rotating the whole vase tilted its *straight* base along with it, so the mark read as **falling over**. A straight line, tilted, implies tilted ground. Round 6 fixed it by keeping the lean and curving the foot — a curved base has no implied ground line, so the lean reads as the vase leaning rather than the world tipping.

## Approving a design is not the same as shipping it

The shape was locked, ported to Dart, and rendered on device 20% too wide.

<Figure
  items={[
    {
      src: "/blog/potter-logo/12-aspect-distortion.png",
      caption:
        "Round 12 — approved (left), what Flutter was actually rendering (middle), overlaid (right). Terracotta is the approved shape. The stretch reads hardest in the neck.",
    },
  ]}
/>

The design space is 100×120 — a 5:6 ratio. The porting instructions scaled the axes independently (`sx = width/100`, `sy = height/120`) while the widget passed a *square* box, so at `Size(120, 120)` the x axis scaled by 1.2 and the y by 1.0. Measured bounds aspect: **0.699** against the approved **0.582**. Every preview I'd approved came from an SVG `viewBox="0 0 100 120"`, which preserves the ratio. The Dart port silently did not.

Two things about how it was caught matter more than the bug:

- **The golden test couldn't catch it.** A golden captures whatever the code produces, so it locked in the wrong shape just as happily as the right one and would have passed forever after.
- **The code review didn't catch it either.** It flagged that the vertical-centering term was always exactly zero and graded that Minor. That dead term was a *symptom* of the same broken formula — but the review stopped at the arithmetic without asking what the shape actually came out looking like.

What found it was measuring the rendered bounds and comparing them against the design's ratio. The fix scales by `min(w/100, h/120)` and centres on both axes, so the mark letterboxes into any box and no caller can distort it:

```dart
Path buildVasePath(Size size) {
  // One factor for both axes: the 5:6 design space must not stretch to fit a
  // square box. The offsets letterbox the mark inside whatever size is given.
  final s = math.min(size.width / 100, size.height / 120);
  final ox = (size.width - 100 * s) / 2;
  final oy = (size.height - 120 * s) / 2;

  double x(double v) => v * s + ox;
  double y(double v) => v * s + oy;
  ...
```

That function is the source of truth for the shape. The splash animation traces it, and the app icon is generated by rendering it — there's no exported PNG that can fall out of date, because the icon and the animation are literally the same path.

## Making it draw itself

First build on the simulator. Three complaints, all mine: the animation drew "from both sides", it felt slow, and it cut too abruptly into the app.

<Figure
  items={[
    {
      src: "/blog/potter-logo/15-drawon-first.png",

"From both sides" was easy: the rim ellipse started near the right lip at the same moment the body started at the left. Sequence the subpaths and it goes away.

The second complaint was that the neck looked like it got **drawn twice**, and that one turned out to be structural. The body is one continuous outline, so tracing it travels down the left side, across the base, and back up the right. The vase's neck is two nearly-parallel lines — and they sit at *opposite ends* of the timeline, roughly 600ms apart. At speed, the second one reads as the first being redrawn.

No amount of re-timing a single travelling stroke fixes that. It's inherent to tracing a silhouette. What fixes it is splitting the body into its two profiles and growing them **together**, so paired features appear at the same moment. Round 19's variant J rises from the base to the lip, which also happens to be the right metaphor for a pottery app — it reads as a vessel being pulled up on a wheel.

Round 20 re-rooted the rim ellipse from its rightmost point `(64, 9.8)` to its **leftmost** `(36, 9.9)` — the same point where the body begins — so both strokes leave one place and diverge, the rim sweeping up over the top while the body descends. One gesture splitting, rather than two marks appearing. Same round settled the easing at `easeInOutQuart`, a much more pronounced slow-fast-slow than Flutter's default `easeInOut`.

Then the footring, which took four rounds on its own.

It still read as a separate mark. The obvious fix — make it part of the pot's stroke — is impossible: the footring is a chord between `(33,100)` and `(67,100)`, two points the body outline *already passes through*. Joining them would mean the pen doubling back over ground it had covered. It's a detail line, not part of the silhouette.

What actually made it feel disconnected was timing. It ran its own 750ms clock, so it finished long before the body's stroke arrived at the base. The fix was to drive every subpath from one clock and gate the footring on the body's own progress — measure where the body path crosses those two points, get a window of **41% → 59%** of its length, and draw the ring only across that window. It now appears under the pen as it sweeps by:

```dart
/// Where along the body subpath the stroke passes the footring's two ends.
///
/// The footring is a chord between two points the body outline already travels
/// through, so it cannot be drawn as part of that stroke without doubling back.
/// Instead it is gated on these fractions, which makes the ring appear under the
/// pen as it sweeps the base rather than running on its own clock.
const _footringWindowStart = 0.41;
const _footringWindowEnd = 0.59;
```

That also reversed an earlier decision. Round 21 had made the footring draw right-to-left, as deliberate counterpoint against three left-to-right strokes, and at the time that was correct. Once the ring is gated on the pen's crossing, drawing it right-to-left runs it *against* the stroke it's supposed to be following — closing toward the pen instead of trailing it. Round 23 put it back. A decision stops being valid when its premise changes.

## The 450 milliseconds of nothing

Third complaint: the cut into the app. I added a fade to the destination route, and it didn't help. Capturing the handoff at 30fps showed why.

<Figure
  cols={1}
  items={[

      caption:
        "the mark lifts away cleanly, then roughly 450ms of blank cream, then the app appears in a single frame",
    },
    {
      src: "/blog/potter-logo/27-overlay-crossfade.png",
      label: "After",
      caption:
        "the journal is visible through the fading mark — both on screen at the same time",
    },
  ]}
  caption="Two captures of the same handoff at 30fps. Everything between the two was architecture, not timing."
/>

The route fade *was* running. It was fading in an **empty** album — the album's data stream only starts when the album mounts, so the page had nothing to paint during its own transition and the content popped in afterwards. A transition can't rescue a screen that isn't ready.

Subscribing to the album's query from the splash, while the mark is still being drawn, cut the gap from ~450ms to ~330ms. But a gap remained, and it was architectural: the splash was a **route**, so the app could not exist until the splash stopped existing. Every frame of blank cream was Flutter building the shell and album *after* the splash was gone.

The splash is now an **overlay**. `MaterialApp.builder` stacks it above the router, so the app builds, redirects and loads its data underneath while the mark is still being drawn. Lifting the overlay reveals a screen that was already there.

In order, the fixes I tried: add a route transition (no effect — it was fading in an empty page); pre-warm the album's query (450 → 330ms); add motion to the entry (no effect — the gap was *before* the transition); make the splash an overlay (gap gone). The first three were treating a symptom.

## What I'd take to the next one

TK these are the archive's own "what generalizes" notes, reworded — they're the agent's conclusions about the process, not yours. Cut the ones you don't actually believe and reorder the rest; a list of seven that you half-agree with is worse than a list of three that you do.

- **Render before asking.** Round 1 was presented to me as "these aren't good enough" rather than as a choice, because seeing them made the flaw obvious. A verbal description of a vase silhouette is nearly worthless. The render takes seconds and settles it.
- **Comparisons beat sequences.** The round 7-vs-8 page and the 2× foot crop each revealed something that full-size, one-at-a-time review had not. Two rounds existing only so they can sit next to each other is a good use of a round.
- **Ask what a rough edge is doing before sanding it.** Round 8 smoothed away the footring that made round 7 correct.
- **Constraints hide in the smallest gap.** Nobody predicts that a decorative footring line sets the maximum stroke weight for the whole mark, at both sizes.
- **A reference image is worth many rounds.** Six rounds of approximating, then one photo made the target unambiguous.
- **Approving a design doesn't mean it shipped.** The port to code silently changed the proportions, and neither the golden test nor the code review caught it. For a shape, verifying means measuring the rendered dimensions — not reading the code that produces them.
- **Verify with the toolchain that ships.** One device check went wrong in a way that looked exactly like a critical bug in this feature: the app sat on a blank cream screen forever. It wasn't the animation. An unsigned simulator build has no keychain entitlement, so the encrypted database can't read its key and `main()` throws before `runApp` is ever called. `flutter run` signs the app; a hand-installed unsigned build loses entitlements and fails in ways that impersonate application bugs.
